# Milestone 5F: Deterministic 3D Combat and Orbital Pivot Prototype (Revision B + arena-edge correction)

Revision A replaces the unaccepted parallel-depth-lane model. This remains Milestone 5F. The arena has authoritative integer X/Y/Z fighter coordinates, but the fighter pair defines a transient local horizontal combat axis. There is no privileged world-Z-zero fighting plane and no automatic world-Z recentering. Pivot can create a slight diagonal relationship that persists; the next committed interaction uses that line as the readable fighting axis. Ordinary play remains effectively one-dimensional along the pair axis, without free 3D locomotion.

## Simulation and geometry

All authoritative positions and velocities remain signed integer subunits (1024 per world unit) at fixed 60 Hz. `FMatchState` remains trivially copyable. A transient axis comes from the integer fighter-to-opponent X/Z delta, integer square root, and division into a signed Q14 unit vector (scale 16384). Deltas are bounded to the legal arena span before squaring, including for malformed extreme initial coordinates. Zero-distance fallback uses logical `Facing`. No authoritative floating-point vector, angle, camera state, physics, or `DeltaTime` is involved. Derived axes are computed from both fighters' start-of-frame snapshots before stepping either fighter. An attack captures its local axis on commitment because changing its trajectory during startup/active frames would erase geometric Pivot whiffs; those two integer components are rollback state. Integer Z velocity, committed jump Z velocity, and pending reaction Z velocity are also explicit rollback fields.

Walking, dashing, and directional jumping follow the current local horizontal line. Left/Right retain their world-screen semantics for the old Z=0 case. Hit knockback and Block pushback keep their previous magnitudes but project along the attacker-to-defender axis at contact. Body push applies only to strict 3D-overlapping grounded hurt volumes and separates along their local horizontal relationship; separated depth faces do not push. The visible and playable horizontal arena remains a circular disc centered at X/Z = 0 with radius **480 world units = 491,520 integer subunits**. The authoritative hurtbox has half-extents 24 units in X and 5 units in Z, so legal fighter centers are constrained to a conservative 455-unit circle: the 25-unit radial footprint margin is the integer ceiling of the hurtbox corner radius. This keeps the complete rectangular hurtbox inside the unchanged 480-unit floor. The same integer projection constrains walking, dash, aerial momentum, Pivot, reactions, and body push. It caps malformed intended coordinates to int32 before uint64 squaring, leaves legal and exact-boundary centers unchanged, and projects outside centers by dividing both coordinates by the ceiling integer square root of their squared distance. Component truncation always lands on or inside the legal center circle. Committed outward radial momentum is removed without a bounce while tangent/inward motion remains available. The collision-disabled greybox body and facing marker fit this deterministic footprint. The disc has no privileged combat orientation; normal control grammar remains effectively 2D along the pair axis, without free 3D locomotion.

`FSimBox` remains the strict integer world-aligned 3D hurt volume. Attacks use `FSimOrientedBox`: an integer vertical interval and a fixed-point oriented horizontal rectangle. Contact tests use strict four-axis integer separating-axis projections, so touching faces miss and a diagonal volume does not gain false coverage from its enclosing world AABB. `GetMoveHitbox` exposes that enclosing AABB only for compatibility; it does not decide contact. The immutable normal definitions retain their X/Y geometry, frame timing, damage, Hit/Block effects, and guard heights. Revision A retunes only prototype lateral half-extents: hurt 5, Light 4, Medium 30, Heavy 6 world units. These widths allow a small orbital Pivot to miss a narrow committed attack while broad Medium can still catch it. They are prototype geometry, not final balance or semantic AoE flags.

## Orbital Pivot

The two existing frame-input bits and Q/E debug controls now mean counter-clockwise/clockwise around the opponent, viewed in the simulation X/Z plane. A fresh press from grounded Idle, Walking, or Crouching starts a 12-frame committed Pivot. Attack, Block, jump, dash, reaction, hitstop, second Pivot, reversal, and input buffering remain unavailable during it. Pivot has no invulnerability. Its first nine frames use angle weights `4, 6, 8, 10, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2` out of 58; its last three frames have zero motion but remain committed. The total target is approximately 8 degrees. Each frame rotates the current fighter-to-opponent integer vector with Q20 sine/cosine polynomial terms, then projects the resulting X/Z point onto the circular disc if needed. No floating-point trig or accumulated authoritative angle is used. Integer rounding, opponent movement, and circle projection mean the achieved angle and radius can vary slightly; arena legality takes precedence. Pivot ends without snapping or drifting to Z=0.

Valid contact still classifies both directions before applying Hit or Block. Pivot success is the transient geometric result `None`, not an invulnerability flag. A Pivot that remains within an active attack volume takes a full Hit; holding Away during Pivot does not auto-Block. Block remains the concessionary baseline defence. Pivot and future Parry are situational. Parry and Pulse Combo remain unimplemented.

## Debug presentation

Presentation maps simulation X/Z/Y to Unreal X/Y/Z. A thin cylinder mesh forms a collision-disabled circular floor disc with a 480-unit visual radius. The greybox body and facing marker fit inside the simulation 48-by-10-unit X/Z hurtbox footprint. The fighters turn toward one another using presentation-only yaw. The camera has no downward pitch and can view any accumulated combat-axis orientation around the circular arena; it has no fixed ±12-degree world-X clamp. The current simulation pair axis and the currently presented combat plane are separate during Pivot. Before each simulation step in ordinary play, the presented camera yaw is aligned to the current pair axis. On entry to either fighter's Pivot, the driver retains the displayed yaw and cancels any unfinished recenter. It holds that orientation for the complete Pivot state, including the three recovery frames, while camera position may continue tracking the pair center using the held yaw. When the last Pivot exits, the driver captures the new pair-axis camera yaw and smoothly interpolates to it over 10 presentation frames (1/6 second at 60 FPS) using smoothstep and the shortest wrapped angular difference. Another Pivot during this transition uses the current displayed yaw, cancels the transition, holds through Pivot, then targets the newest pair axis. This camera state is owned by `ADebugMatchDriver`; it is not authoritative, snapshotted, or used to gate simulation, and it never changes fighter coordinates. Cyan and yellow debug lines show the live pair axis and presented combat plane respectively. Green wire boxes show hurt volumes and red oriented wire boxes show active attacks. The HUD shows XYZ position, XYZ velocity, orbital direction, and reaction X/Z; top-screen health bars still read authoritative simulation health and are presentation-only. No production animation, AI, VFX, or new gameplay family is included.
